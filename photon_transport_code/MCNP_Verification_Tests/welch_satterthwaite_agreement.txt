1. WHY WELCH-SATTERTHWAITE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The two codes do not estimate sigma the same way, and the two estimates do
not carry the same precision:

  MC/DC   sigma is a batch-to-batch sample standard error over B batches.
          Every one of the eight comparison problems ran B = 10, so that
          estimate has nu_M = B - 1 = 9 degrees of freedom.  sigma itself
          is uncertain by ~1/sqrt(2(B-1)) = 24%.

  MCNP    sigma is a per-history estimate over the full nps.  MCNP has no
          user batch count -- one continuous run -- so nu_N = nps - 1.
          Here nps >= 1e6 in every problem, so nu_N is effectively
          infinite and sigma_N is effectively exact.

Comparing two means whose variance estimates have unequal and finite dof is
the Behrens-Fisher problem.  A plain z-test against 1.96 implicitly treats
BOTH sigmas as exact, which over-credits MC/DC's 10-batch estimate and
rejects agreement too often.  The Welch-Satterthwaite effective dof of the
pooled variance is

    nu_eff = (s_M^2 + s_N^2)^2 / ( s_M^4/nu_M + s_N^4/nu_N )

and a bin is called statistically indistinguishable when

    |t| = |phi_M - phi_N| / sqrt(s_M^2 + s_N^2)  <=  t(0.975, nu_eff)

Because nu_M = 9 is small, nu_eff is pulled toward 9 in every bin where
MC/DC's sigma contributes appreciably, and the critical value rises from
1.960 toward t(0.975, 9) = 2.262.  The bound is therefore

    1.960  <=  t_crit  <=  2.262

so this test can only ever call MORE bins indistinguishable than the
z-test, never fewer.  Both counts are reported side by side below.

For the two Al sphere problems no MCNP output file survives.  Per the run
record both used nps = 1e6, and MCNP reports per-history error, so nu_N =
1e6 - 1 is assumed for them.  t(0.975, nu) is flat to 4 decimals for any
nu above ~1e5, so this assumption changes no verdict.


2. BIN EXCLUSIONS (applied symmetrically to both codes)
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A bin with zero flux in either code has an UNDEFINED relative error, not a
zero one -- MCNP prints 0.0000 for an empty bin and MC/DC can print a zero
sdev when all batch scores coincide.  Such bins carry no information about
agreement and are dropped from both the numerator and the denominator.
Two further exclusions, both carried over from the paper's own comparison
script:

  * MM Sphere Spectrum, energy bin 1 (0.0100-0.0119 MeV): MCNP's first
    tally interval starts at 0 MeV, MC/DC's at 0.01 MeV -- not the same bin.
  * MM Slab Mesh Tally, z = -0.5 cm: the vacuum backing gap behind the
    source, outside the depth range the paper plots.

4. OVERALL AGREEMENT -- THREE INTERVALS, AND WHICH TO QUOTE
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(a) Pooled bin-level, every comparable bin treated as one independent draw

      244 / 702 = 34.8%      95% CI  [31.3%, 38.4%]   (Wilson)
                             95% CI  [31.2%, 38.4%]   (Clopper-Pearson)

    This is the headline number, and it is the one directly comparable to
    the claim currently in the abstract.  Its interval is however too
    narrow: bins within one problem share a geometry, a source and a
    cross-section path, so they are not independent draws, and just two
    problems (mesh tally and sphere spectrum) supply 620 of the 702 bins,
    i.e. 88% of the pooled weight.

(b) Equal weight per problem -- the mean of the eight per-problem rates

      mean = 41.4%   sd = 35.4%   95% CI  [11.8%, 71.0%]   (t, 7 dof)

    This treats the eight benchmark problems as the sampling unit rather
    than the bins, so one 420-bin problem cannot outvote one 4-bin
    problem.  The interval is wide because the per-problem rates are
    genuinely spread from 0% to 100% -- that spread is the real result.

(c) Pooled, corrected for clustering by problem (design effect)

      design effect = 25.3   effective n = 28 of 702 bins
      34.8%      95% CI  [20.7%, 54.2%]

    The between-problem spread inflates the variance by a factor of
    25.3 over binomial, so the 702 bins carry only about 28 bins' worth
    of independent information.  Treat this as evidence that interval
    (a) is too narrow rather than as a precise interval itself: with
    only 8 clusters, of sizes 4 to 420, the design effect is a noisy
    estimate.  For a headline claim, quote (a) for the literal bin
    fraction and (b) when speaking about the problems as a set.


5. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE PAPER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The abstract currently states that 'roughly 55% of individual tally bins
are statistically indistinguishable between MC/DC and MCNP' at the 95%
confidence level.  Recomputed over all 702 comparable bins with the Welch-
Satterthwaite correction, the figure is 34.8%, 95% CI [31.3%, 38.4%]
pooled, or 41.4% [11.8%, 71.0%] weighting each problem equally.

The Welch correction moves the pooled count by +11 bins (33.2% -> 34.8%)
relative to the z-test, so the gap against 55% is not an artefact of the
unequal batch structure: it survives the correction that was supposed to
explain it.  The dominant cause is the mesh tally, where a persistent
5-12% flux offset sits far outside sigmas of ~1.4%, and the mesh tally
alone contributes 200 of the 702 bins.

Two honest framings are available and both should be stated:
  * pooled over bins, agreement is 34.8% -- driven down by the two
    large-bin-count problems;
  * per problem, agreement ranges from 0.0% to 100.0%, and the simple
    average across the eight problems is 41.4%.

Note separately that 'statistically indistinguishable' and 'physically
close' are different claims.  MM Slab Collim Beam agrees in 0 of 4 bins
yet its largest flux difference is 8.24%, because its sigmas are ~0.16%;
Pb 10 MeV agrees in 12 of 16 bins while differing by up to 76%, because
its sigmas reach 100%.  A high agreement fraction in a high-variance
problem is weak evidence of physics agreement, not strong evidence.
