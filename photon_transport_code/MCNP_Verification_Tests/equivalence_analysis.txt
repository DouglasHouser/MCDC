1. WHY A SECOND METRIC IS NEEDED
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The companion report answers 'what fraction of bins are statistically
INDISTINGUISHABLE?'.  That is a significance test of H0: phi_M = phi_N, and
as a verification figure of merit it runs backwards:

  * Precision punishes you.  As histories -> inf, sigma -> 0, so |t| -> inf
    for ANY nonzero bias, however physically trivial.  The mesh tally has
    sigma ~1.4 % against a 5-12 % offset and scores 1.5 %.  Running MORE
    histories would push that fraction toward 0 %.
  * Noise rewards you.  Bins whose sigma reach 100 % 'agree' because
    nothing can be resolved there at all.

Worse, failing to reject H0 is not evidence FOR H0.  A low agreement
fraction is therefore not evidence that MC/DC is wrong, and a high one
would not have been evidence that it is right.

The claim the paper wants is equivalence within a tolerance -- the reverse
hypothesis:

    H0: |phi_M/phi_N - 1| >= delta     (codes differ by at least delta)
    H1: |phi_M/phi_N - 1| <  delta     (codes agree within delta)

Rejecting THIS H0 is positive evidence of agreement, and precision now
helps instead of hurting.  That is the TOST procedure, implemented here as
a CI-containment test on the flux ratio:

    R = phi_M/phi_N,   s_lnR = sqrt(re_M^2 + re_N^2)
    nu_eff = (re_M^2+re_N^2)^2 / (re_M^4/nu_M + re_N^4/nu_N)
    CI(R) = exp( ln R +/- t(0.975, nu_eff) * s_lnR )


2. THREE-WAY VERDICT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A pass/fail split would hide the distinction that matters most here, so
each bin gets one of three verdicts against tolerance delta:

  EQUIVALENT    CI(R) lies entirely inside [1-delta, 1+delta].
                Agreement within delta is DEMONSTRATED.
  DIFFERENT     CI(R) lies entirely outside the band.
                A discrepancy larger than delta is DEMONSTRATED.
  INCONCLUSIVE  CI(R) straddles a boundary.  The run is too noisy to
                decide at this delta -- not a defect, just no information.

Separating INCONCLUSIVE from DIFFERENT is the whole point: under the
indistinguishability metric those two were both counted as 'agreement',
which is why noisy problems scored well and precise ones scored badly.

5. HOW TO STATE THIS IN THE PAPER
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Defensible, and all three from the same analysis:

  * Mean absolute flux difference across all 695 comparable bins is 6.3 %,
    median 2.1 %.
  * 69.1% of bins are demonstrably equivalent within 10 % at 95 %
    confidence; 58.6% within 5 %.
  * 34.8% of bins are statistically indistinguishable, but this metric is
    depressed by the high-precision runs and should be reported WITH the
    equivalence result, not instead of it.

Avoid: 'results within 5 % of MCNP' stated as a bound.  It is not a bound
-- the largest differences reach 94 %.  It is true of the MEAN.
